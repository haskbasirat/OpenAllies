# Film corpus census

Generated by `aa-parity corpus` over the unique real films (by sha256). 62 films: 58 decode on a data set computed from game data, 3 decode only with reference widths because their data is not in the corpus, 1 without a chunk directory cannot be opened (the game never closed them). 1818301 world ticks, 909865 decrees, 0 records that do not decode to their exact end.

## What is proven

- Decoding: a record counts as decoded only when the native descriptor loop (0x74D710 order) ends exactly on its last bit and every definition index is below its live count (0x411FA0 possibilities, rejected otherwise).
- Decree identity, against the recording: the film's own 311 checkpoints hash every S-logging decree's name and id. Of 52730 checkpoint intervals, 2354 hold only clock and decree entries, so their hash can be recomputed from the decode alone: 2354 of them equal the recorded hash (1 film(s) of a saved game fit at the clock offset their intervals agree on). 0 intervals hold fewer entries than the decoded decrees account for.
- Counts on v14: the computed ActorCommand, ThingData, Formation, OrganizationLayout, SharedProperty and AbilityDefinition counts equal what NativeSampler read from the running original, and the SharedProperty slots equal its 194 live IDS in order (`tests/registry_counts.rs`).
- Counts on the mods: computed by the same rules, with no live reading to compare. The evidence is that the films recorded on them decode, their header ids exist, and the references name what they should (the last two columns of the data set table).
- The AbilityDefinition slot order has no live reading to compare; the evidence is the films: 3031 of 3031 70 FactionUseAbility decrees on named data sets name one of the ordering player's own faction abilities.
- Human or computer is inferred: a kingdom renamed by 328 UpdateKingdomName is a computer player's (SessionPlayers leaves out the `ai` flag, 76C310 only logs it). A session player off the film's roster was created by 329 KKC_MidgameCreateAIsAndSessionPlayers and counts as a computer; a rename after a 329 is that AI taking over and leaves the human who left as human. The selection decrees agree: humans use 16 and 18, computers 333.

## Failure causes

Every failure of the first pass had one of these causes, all fixed in the reader:

- A film of a saved game (8 films) has its creator's rules in mode `savegame`, which aa-mapworld refuses although 0x512420 only stores the word. `film::decode_film_header` reads them.
- 23 KKC_TeamCommand (314 decrees) needs the flags of its definition. They are code-defined (0x475590) and now known: `registries::TEAM_COMMANDS`.
- Every other record that stopped had its references read at the wrong width, because the counts of its data were not those of v14. A mod's ThingData takes 14 bits against 11. The counts now come from the data the film was recorded with.
- 3 films are on a data set the corpus does not hold (`widths`): their references fit the stock widths, but some index is above the v14 count, so they decode only with counts opened to the width. Their definition references have no names here.
- 1 film(s) were never closed: no chunk directory, so the original cannot open them. The zlib streams are all there except the end of Orders.
- F54 is a copy of such a film with a rebuilt directory and its complete records (16058 ticks), made to count its orders. The original cannot open the film it comes from.

## The film version check (0x5A3980) on the v14 target

With `FilmForcePlayback` false (UVars default) the original compares the header's version string (+0x58, the recording game's `Version` var) with its own by `wcscmp`, then the build (+0x50) with 4129.7 as binary32, and throws `failed_film_wrong_version|s` or `failed_film_wrong_build|g` before CreateGWorld. The v14 target has version 1.0.5.0 and build 4129.7, like every AA.exe of the project.

## Sources: a profile's data and saved games

A film holds no map. What it needs lies beside it, in the data folder of the profile it lives in (lead's decision 6). `aa_parity::playback` now finds all of it. `tests/film_sources.rs` holds the checks.

**A film on a profile's data.** `FilmData::for_film` takes `<profile>/data` as the overlay when the film lies in its `Films` folder. A folder with an `index*.lst` holds definitions of its own; a folder without one holds only what the game wrote there, and the definitions stay the game's. On definitions of its own:

- `V14Sources::open_over_folder` reads the loose folder over the archive, by lowercase path, without the v14 overlay. `GameData`, `GeneralData`, `RandomMapData` and `Registries` load on it through `DataFolder`, and the results are cached by a key made from every file's path, size and time, leaving out what the game writes while it plays (films, saves, screenshots).
- `GameMap::load_file` reads an RMP the way the original does, without the extracted JSON. A map the folder holds also brings its own triggers (`triggers::attach_bytes`) and its SUN chunk.
- `AssetStore::set_art_overlay` lays the folder's art over the stock art for the length of the battle. A mod holds about 9,000 NIFs and 20,000 textures and a battle draws a few hundred, so `aa_assets::convert::FolderArt` converts a model or a texture when it is first asked for and keeps the result in `<own profile>/art/<key>` for the next battle. A file the folder holds byte for byte like the stock data stays the stock conversion. No Python is involved, so a release draws a mod's art on its first film. `aa_assets::convert` is the project's own exporter (the one that made the stock `assets/converted`) on the ported NiStream reader. `tests/convert_stock.rs` holds it against the whole stock art: see Art below.
- The same battle's effects, sounds and interface text come from the folder too (Art below).

**Art.** The Rust exporter against the stock conversion, `tests/convert_stock.rs`: of the 1,900 stock NIFs, the same 53 fail with the same reason and the other 1,847 convert value for value (the three later notes on the stock files aside: `baseTextureState`, `alphaPropertyState`, `textureAlias`). The 11,035 meshes of the material audit get the same tags, alpha and depth state and sphere centre, so a folder's model is corrected at its conversion and needs no audit. The stock audit stays for the stock art. The 5,825 stock DDS and TGA textures that made a PNG of their own equal it pixel by pixel (a 16 bit TGA may differ by one step: its five bit channels widen by another rounding). On a real mod, every third file: 2,848 of 2,980 NIFs are its own, 27 do not convert (19 hold no drawn geometry, the rest are damaged), a texture takes about 27 ms.

A battle on a folder also reads, from the folder first: the effect tables of its catalog and the effect NIFs and their textures (`effects::preload_on`, cached per folder by what it holds), every NIF the client reads for bones and clips (`effects::bones::find_file`), the sounds (`audio::Files`), the ground maps, and the interface text (`StringTable::add_folder`, for the length of the battle). Not yet: the banners, namelists and shadow types (read once for the stock data) and a mod's own rigs (`assets-cache/animation`, made by the project's exporter for the stock models): a model of the folder without a rig draws in its bind pose.

**Closed sides of a random map.** Some creators list more sides than the session plays: a film with `side01`, `side02`, `side03`, `side05`. A kingdom's +0x188 is its place in the RMC's list, so the header's order is wrong for these films. The generator now takes the session's sides by name (`WorldSpec::present`) and the film's players give the indices. The creator's other sides still make their world-start draws. Whether the original also generates them and then leaves them out is not known yet.

**A film of a saved game (creator mode 2).** `aa_mapworld::saved_world` walks every record of a save with the original's readers, an organization's embedded members included. `aa_play::saved_world::restore` builds the world from it, and a Load Game can use the same function later. Its `Restored::notes` say what is left out, and the film keeps them (`FilmPlayer::notes`).

Restored:

- the clock
- the world stream, with the record pass's draws
- the GTIDs
- kingdoms: stockpiles, relations, properties, generals, experience and ability recharges
- things: position, facing and health
- organizations: members by slot, morale, experience and attack efficiency
- production queues
- the Supply's stock
- the periodic and deferred queues (`UPDATABLES`)
- Minds (`saved_world::apply_saved_minds`): stack, pending and current commands, the Move state with its path, the fighting states the Mind hosts (Kill, Chasing, Reform, Engage with its list), the Mover's speed and destination, the Character's mode. A saved GTID in a Mind word or a command becomes what the hosted Mind names a thing by
- what `crate::combat` and `crate::morale` still hold of a regiment's Mind: the rout, the exhausted state, the entrenched and exhausted state bits
